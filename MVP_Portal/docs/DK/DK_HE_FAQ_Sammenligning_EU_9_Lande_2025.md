# Denmark – Sundhedsundervisning: FAQ, Context and 9-Country Comparative Analysis (2025)

## 1. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q: Does Denmark have a dedicated "Health Education" subject?**  
A: No. Health education is distributed as a mandatory *emne* (topic) – *sundheds- og seksualundervisning og familiekundskab* – across multiple subjects, plus *Idræt* (PE) and *Biologi*. No dedicated subject with its own hours exists.

**Q: Is sex education mandatory in Denmark?**  
A: Yes – since **1970** in folkeskole. From **2023/24**, extended to all gymnasium programmes. Sex education has been mandatory for 55 years at the lower level.

**Q: How many hours of sex education do Danish students receive?**  
A: This is **not regulated by law**. The topic (*emne*) has no minimum hours requirement. Implementation depends entirely on the school principal's decision about which subjects carry the content and how many hours to allocate. This is the primary structural weakness of the Danish model.

**Q: What happened with abortion law reform in 2023?**  
A: In 2023, Denmark extended the legal abortion limit from 12 to **22 weeks** – one of the most progressive abortion laws in Europe. This is now included in gymnasium health education curriculum.

**Q: What is Sex og Samfund?**  
A: *Sex og Samfund* (Sex and Society) is Denmark's leading sexuality education NGO, founded 1956. It provides:
- Professional sex educators visiting schools (*seksualvejledere*)
- Teacher training
- The *Sexlinien* (free anonymous phone/online advice service)
- Curriculum materials aligned with Fælles Mål
- Advocacy for mandatory, quality sex education

**Q: What is DANMAP?**  
A: The Danish Integrated Antimicrobial Resistance Monitoring and Research Programme – a joint initiative monitoring antibiotic use simultaneously in humans, animals and food production since 1995. It is a global model for One Health AMR surveillance and influences biology curriculum content in Denmark.

**Q: What are the biggest youth health challenges in Denmark (2022)?**  
A: According to HBSC 2022:
1. **Socioeconomic inequality in mental health** – rapidly widening gap since 2014; girls from disadvantaged families hardest hit (46.1% low mental well-being)
2. **Post-COVID mental health burden** – 18% increase in psychotropic medication use 5–24 year olds
3. **Widening obesity inequality** – 17.6% overweight among disadvantaged vs. 9.0% advantaged
4. **E-cigarettes** – growing use across genders
5. **Screen time and physical inactivity** – below WHO activity recommendations

## 2. Danish Contextual Specificities

### 2.1 The Danish Welfare State Model and Health Education

Denmark's comprehensive welfare state (*velfærdsstat*) shapes health education:
- **Free healthcare (sygesikring)**: Universal GP access; all health services free at point of use
- **Free contraception** for under-18s (since 2023 – previously under-15)
- **Free abortion** on demand to 22 weeks (from 2023)
- **High health literacy** expected of citizens: welfare state model requires informed participants
- **Sundhedsplejerske**: school health nurse in every school (municipally funded)

### 2.2 The Problem of Hours: "Frihed under ansvar" (Freedom with Responsibility)

The core structural tension in Danish health education:

> Danish schools have **great pedagogical freedom** but **no minimum hour requirement** for health/sex education → creates quality inequality between schools.

This "freedom with responsibility" (*frihed under ansvar*) is a core Danish educational philosophy applied across the curriculum, but creates documented uneven outcomes in health education specifically.

### 2.3 Chlamydia in Denmark: High Detection ≠ High Prevalence

A methodologically important distinction for comparative work:

Denmark has high reported **chlamydia rates** (among highest in EU). However, this is significantly because:
- Denmark has normalised, easily accessible, *free* STI testing
- Young people are encouraged to test annually
- High detection = high testing culture, **not necessarily** higher prevalence

This demonstrates how health education approach (normalising testing, reducing stigma) directly affects epidemiological statistics.

### 3.2 Denmark's Distinctive Position

**Strengths:**
- DANMAP – global model for One Health AMR surveillance; influences education
- Abortion law reform 2023 (to 22 weeks) – among most progressive in Europe; in curriculum
- Free contraception for under-18s (extended 2023)
- Sex og Samfund – 60+ years of professional sex education support
- High STI testing culture (normalised; free; reducing stigma)

**Weaknesses:**
- **No minimum hours** for sex education – creates significant quality variance
- No dedicated HE subject → hard to guarantee coverage
- No nationwide youth health clinic network (unlike Sweden's ungdomsmottagning)
- Widening socioeconomic inequality in adolescent mental health (HBSC 2022)
