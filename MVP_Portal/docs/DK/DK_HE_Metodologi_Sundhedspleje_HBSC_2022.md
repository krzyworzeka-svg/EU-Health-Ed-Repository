# Denmark – Health Education: Methodology, School Health and Youth Health Statistics (2022–2025)

## 1. Pedagogical Approaches in Danish Health and Sex Education

### 1.1 Core Philosophy: "Livsduelighed" (Life Competency)

Danish education broadly emphasises *livsduelighed* – the ability to cope with and navigate life. Applied to health education, this means:
- Health literacy as **practical competency**, not just knowledge
- Ability to make informed, autonomous decisions
- Building resilience and self-efficacy in health contexts
- Critical thinking about health information sources (media, internet)

### 1.2 Methods Used in Sundheds- og seksualundervisning

| Method | Description |
|---|---|
| **Dialogue-based teaching** | Open discussion; teacher creates safe space for sharing |
| **Case studies** | Real-life scenarios (anonymised); health decision-making analysis |
| **Role play** | Practising conversations about consent, setting limits |
| **Group projects** | Research into health topics relevant to pupils |
| **Sex og Samfund visits** | Professional sex educators visiting schools (NGO) |
| **Digital platforms** | Online tools: *Sexlinien* (chat/phone advice); *Smittestop* (STI awareness) |
| **Peer education** | Older pupils as peer educators (some municipalities) |
| **Media analysis** | Critically analysing pornography, advertising, social media norms |

### 1.3 The Sex og Samfund Model

**Sex og Samfund** (Sex and Society) is Denmark's key NGO for sex education:
- Provides professional *seksualvejledere* (sex educators) to visit schools
- Produces major teacher training programmes
- Operates the *Sexlinien* service: free, anonymous phone/chat line on sexual health questions
- Produces curricula materials aligned with Fælles Mål
- Runs the "Cybernoter" programme: cybersexuality and digital consent for teens
- Partners with Sundhedsstyrelsen on national campaigns

### 1.4 Structural Challenge: Uneven Implementation

A major challenge in the Danish model is **uneven quality of implementation**:
- No minimum hours mandated → some schools provide very limited coverage
- Heavy dependence on individual teacher confidence
- Quality varies significantly between municipalities and schools
- Rural/urban disparities in availability of support (Sex og Samfund mainly urban)

## 2. School Health Services (Sundhedspleje)

### 2.1 The Sundhedsplejerske (School Health Nurse) System

Denmark's school health system is built around the **sundhedsplejerske** (health visitor/school nurse):

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Legal basis | Sundhedsloven (Health Act); kommunal responsibility |
| Who | Qualified nurses with specialist training in child/school health |
| Coverage | All folkeskole students (grades 0–9) |
| Services | Developmental health checks; vaccination; individual confidential conversations; weight/height monitoring; vision/hearing tests |
| Mental health | Limited – primarily referral to other services |
| Sexual health | Advice; STI info; referral to GP |
| LGBTQ+ support | Variable; depends on individual sundhedsplejerske |

### 2.2 Limitation: No Danish Ungdomsmottagning Equivalent

Unlike Sweden (~270 ungdomsmottagningar), Denmark lacks a nationwide dedicated **youth health clinic** network. Adolescents access sexual/reproductive health through:
- Their **alment praktiserende læge** (GP): free in Denmark
- **Familieplanleggingsklinikker** (family planning clinics) – mainly larger cities
- **STI klinikker** (specific in Copenhagen and major cities)
- The *Sexlinien* and *SMSrådgivning* (digital advice)

## 3. Youth Health Statistics: HBSC Denmark 2022

The Danish HBSC 2022 data is managed by **Statens Institut for Folkesundhed** (SDU) surveying students aged 11, 13 and 15 (N=5,823).

### 3.1 Mental Health – Key 2022 Findings

| Indicator | Value (2022) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Low mental well-being (disadvantaged families) | **33.4%** | Up from 19.7% in 2014 |
| Low mental well-being (advantaged families) | 19.7% | Significant gap |
| Low mental well-being (girls, disadvantaged) | **46.1%** | Alarming; nearly 1 in 2 |
| Low self-esteem (disadvantaged) | **40.9%** | Up from 23.7% in 2014 |
| Low self-esteem (girls, most disadvantaged) | **58.3%** | Majority |
| Increase in psychotropic medication use (5–24 yr) | **+18% post-COVID** | Antidepressants, stimulants |

> **Key finding**: Denmark has a **dramatic socioeconomic gradient** in adolescent mental health that has widened significantly between 2014 and 2022. Girls from disadvantaged backgrounds are disproportionately affected.

### 3.2 Physical Activity and Weight

| Indicator | Value | Trend |
|---|---|---|
| Overweight/obese (advantaged) | 9.0% | ↑ |
| Overweight/obese (disadvantaged) | **17.6%** | ↑ significantly; widening inequality |
| Meeting WHO 60 min/day activity | ~15–20% | Below recommendation |
| Sedentary screen time | Increasing | Concern |

### 3.3 Substance Use

| Substance | Indicator | Value | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alcohol** | % 15-year-olds who have been drunk | ~60–65% | Long-term decline, but plateau |
| **Cannabis** | % 15-year-old boys | Higher than girls | ↑ in recent cycles |
| **E-cigarettes** | Both genders | Growing | ↑ significant new trend |
| **Tobacco** | Declining traditional cigarettes | ↓ | Shift to e-cig |

### 3.4 Sexual Health

| Indicator | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Teen birth rate (15–19/1000) | **~4–5** | Eurostat 2022 (among EU's lowest) |
| Chlamydia rate (15–24) | Among highest in EU (cultural: frequent testing) | SSI 2023 |
| Abortion rate (15–19) | Among lowest in EU | SSI |
| HPV vaccination coverage | ~80–85% (girls and boys) | Sundhedsstyrelsen |

> **Danish-specific note on chlamydia**: Denmark has high chlamydia *detection* rates partly because of normalised, frequent, free STI testing – this does not necessarily indicate higher prevalence, but rather better detection. This is sometimes misread in comparative data.
