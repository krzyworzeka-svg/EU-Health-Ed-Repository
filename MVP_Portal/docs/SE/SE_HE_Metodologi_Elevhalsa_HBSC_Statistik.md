# Sweden – Health Education: Methodology, School Health Services and Youth Health Statistics (2021–2025)

## 1. Pedagogical Approach

### 1.1 Core Philosophy: "Normkritisk pedagogik" (Norm-Critical Pedagogy)

Swedish health education – particularly in the area of sexuality and relationships – is built on **normkritisk pedagogik** (norm-critical pedagogy):

> *Rather than treating the "norm" as neutral and then adding exceptions ("there are also gay people"), norm-critical pedagogy questions who defines the norm, who benefits from it, and whose experiences are rendered invisible.*

In practice, this means:
- **Starting from diversity** as the baseline, not heterosexuality or cisgender identity
- **Critically examining** social expectations, gender roles, and power structures
- Teaching students to **identify and challenge** discrimination and prejudice
- Applying this framework across ALL health-related topics (body image, relationships, substances)

### 1.2 "Kontinuerlig undervisning" (Continuous Teaching)

A key methodological requirement in Sweden is that sexuality, consent and relationships education is **continuous and repeated** – not a one-time event:

- **Not**: a single "sex ed lesson" per year
- **Yes**: recurring age-appropriate engagement across multiple subjects throughout all school years
- The *Skolinspektionen* (School Inspectorate) monitors compliance with this principle

### 1.3 Cross-Subject Collaboration

The required cross-subject approach demands:
- **Planning meetings** between biology, PE, civics and religious studies teachers
- Shared responsibility documented in school plans
- RFSU and other organisations provide teacher support materials and workshops

## 2. School Health Services (Elevhälsan)

### 2.1 Legal Basis

The *elevhälsa* (school health service) is mandated in **Skollagen (School Act 2010:800)**. Every school in Sweden must provide:

| Personnel | Role |
|---|---|
| **Skolsköterska** (School nurse) | Health checks; immunisation; individual consultations; free contraception advice |
| **Skolläkare** (School doctor) | Annual medical assessments; complex health referrals |
| **Psykolog** (Psychologist) | Mental health assessment; crisis intervention; learning difficulties |
| **Kurator** (Welfare officer/Social counsellor) | Social support; family issues; bullying; substance use |
| **Specialpedagog** (Special needs educator) | Learning difficulties; ADHD; dyslexia |

**All services are free for students.** The *elevhälsa* is primarily **preventive** in orientation.

### 2.2 Ungdomsmottagning (Youth Health Clinics)

Beyond the school, Sweden has a nationwide network of **ungdomsmottagningar** (youth health clinics):
- Specifically designed for young people aged ~13–25
- No appointment needed; anonymous possible
- Services: free contraception; STI testing; pregnancy counselling; mental health support; advice on relationships and sexuality
- Run jointly by municipalities and county councils (*regioner*)
- Open model: any young person can access without parental consent
- ~270 clinics nationwide

## 3. Key Pedagogical Methods in Swedish Health Education

| Method | Application |
|---|---|
| **Normkritisk pedagogik** | Questioning who defines "normal"; inclusive approach to diversity |
| **Case-based learning** | Real-world health scenarios (anonymised or fictional) |
| **Group discussion** | Guided discussion on consent, peer pressure, relationships |
| **Individual reflection** | Personal health journals; body awareness exercises |
| **Guest speakers** | RFSU educators; *ungdomsmottagning* nurses; police (on violence) |
| **Media analysis** | Critical analysis of advertising, social media, pornography |
| **Project work** | Group projects on public health issues; sustainability |
| **Digital tools** | Health apps (stegräknare – step counters); pulse monitoring in Idrott oh hälsa |
| **Friluftsliv** | Outdoor activities as health resource; Nordic nature connection |

## 4. Youth Health Statistics: HBSC 2021/2022 (Sverige)

The Swedish HBSC report **"Skolbarns hälsovanor i Sverige 2021/2022"** (published Folkhälsomyndigheten, May 2023) is the primary source.

### 4.1 Mental Health and Well-being

| Indicator | Value (2021/22) | Trend |
|---|---|---|
| % 15-year-old girls reporting schoolwork stress | **78%** (very/quite stressed) | ↑ Significantly since 2013/14 |
| % 15-year-old boys reporting schoolwork stress | **51%** | ↑ Since 2013/14 |
| % students with declining school satisfaction | ↑ since 2013/14 | Negative trend |
| Multiple health complaints (girls) | High; increasing | ↑ vs. 2017/18 |
| Sleep difficulties (adolescents) | Significant concern | Stable/↑ |
| Girls reporting low self-esteem | Higher than boys | ↑ |

> **Key finding**: Sweden has a **significant and growing gender gap** in mental health, with girls reporting substantially higher levels of stress, pressure, and health complaints than boys.

### 4.2 Physical Activity

| Indicator | Value | Trend |
|---|---|---|
| % meeting WHO 60 min/day recommendation | ~15–20% (varies by age/gender) | ↓ below recommendation |
| Girls less active than boys | Significant gap | Persistent |
| Screen time (>4h/day loisir) | ~30–40% of teenagers | ↑ |
| Friluftsliv participation | High compared to EU | Stable; Swedish tradition |

### 4.3 Substance Use

| Substance | Indicator | Value (2021/22) | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alcohol | % 15-year-olds who have been drunk | ~40–50% | ↑ girls (plateau after decline) |
| **Snus** (nicotine pouches/Swedish moist tobacco) | % 15-year-old girls using | **↑ Significant increase** | New pattern |
| Cannabis | % 15-year-old boys who have tried | ↑ | Concerning increase |
| E-cigarettes | Increasing use | ↑ | New trend |

> **Sweden-specific note**: **Snus** (moist snuff) is a culturally specific Swedish/Nordic tobacco product. Its use, particularly among girls, is a uniquely Swedish health education challenge.

### 4.4 Sexual Health (Folkhälsomyndigheten data)

| Indicator | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Teen birth rate (15–19 years/1000) | **~3–4** (among lowest in EU) | Eurostat 2022 |
| Chlamydia (15–24 year olds) | Most common STI; ~30,000 new cases/yr | FHM 2023 |
| HPV vaccination coverage (girls) | ~85–90% | FHM 2023 |
| HPV vaccination coverage (boys) | Programme extended to all 2020; coverage growing | FHM 2023 |
| Access to free contraception (under 21) | **Free in most regions** | Regionalt |

> **Sweden has one of the lowest teenage birth rates in Europe** (~3–4/1000 for 15–19 yr olds vs EU average ~8/1000) – a direct result of 70 years of comprehensive, mandatory sex education and free contraception access.
