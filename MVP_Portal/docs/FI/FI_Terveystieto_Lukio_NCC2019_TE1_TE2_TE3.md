# Finland – Terveystieto (Health Education): Curriculum Content in Lukio (NCC 2019)

## 1. Lukio and the 2019 Reform

### 1.1 Structure of Lukio

**Lukio** (general upper secondary school) in Finland is a voluntary 3-year programme (typically ages 16–19) that prepares students for the **Ylioppilastutkinto** (Finnish Matriculation Examination). The 2019 reform restructured the curriculum from courses to **modules** (opintojaksot), each worth 2 credits (ECTS-like units called op).

**Key features:**
- Students must complete **150 credits** (opintopistettä) total
- Of these, ~118 credits are national compulsory or national elective
- **Health education (Terveystieto)** contributes **6 credits** as compulsory (3 modules × 2 credits) + optional additional modules
- Health education can be taken as a **ylioppilaskoe** (matriculation exam subject) – voluntary but available

### 1.2 Terveystieto Structure in Lukio (NCC 2019)

| Module | Finnish Name | English Name | Credits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TE1** | Terveys voimavarana | Health as a Resource | 2 op (mandatory) |
| **TE2** | Terveys ja ympäristö | Health and Environment | 2 op (mandatory) |
| **TE3** | Terveys ja yhteiskunta | Health and Society | 2 op (mandatory) |
| Optional | Koulun omat moduulit | School-specific optional modules | Variable |

## 2. Module TE1: Terveys voimavarana (Health as a Resource)

### 2.1 Core Philosophy

The **salutogenic approach** underpins this module: health is seen as a **resource** (voimavara) for life, not just the absence of disease. Students develop a holistic, multidimensional view of health: **fyysinen, psyykkinen, sosiaalinen** (physical, mental, social) – and increasingly environmental/ecological.

### 2.2 Learning Objectives

Students will:
- Understand health as a multidimensional, dynamic resource
- Analyse individual, social and environmental factors affecting health
- Develop ability to make evidence-based health decisions
- Strengthen self-care skills and health literacy
- Apply critical thinking to health information sources

### 2.3 Detailed Content

**Health as a Concept:**
- World Health Organization (WHO) definition of health and its evolution
- Salutogenesis (Antonovsky): sense of coherence (koherenssin tunne) as health resource
- Dimensions of health: physical, mental, social, sexual, spiritual, environmental
- Health inequalities: gender, socioeconomic, ethnic differences in health outcomes in Finland

**Lifestyle and Health Promotion:**
- Physical activity in adulthood: WHO recommendations; sedentary behavior risks
- Finnish nutrition recommendations for young adults (Valtion ravitsemusneuvottelukunta)
- Sleep among students: Finnish university students and lukio students; sleep deprivation epidemic
- Substance use in upper secondary: alcohol, tobacco, cannabis, vaping (updated THL Kouluterveyskysely 2023 data)
- Screen time and digital well-being: social media, gaming, learning vs. entertainment

**Mental Health as a Resource:**
- Positive mental health: flourishing, meaning, resilience
- Stress in lukio: exam pressure, future anxiety, social stress
- Burnout among students (*koulu-uupumus*): Finnish research; prevalence; prevention
- Anxiety and depression: distinguishing normal sadness from clinical disorders
- Mental health help in Finland: school nurse, school psychologist, YTHS (Finnish Student Health Service)

**Sexual Health in Adulthood:**
- Sexual health as a lifelong resource: WHO definition
- Healthy relationships: communication, mutual respect, equality, boundaries
- Contraception choices for young adults: advantages/disadvantages of hormonal vs. barrier methods; IUDs; long-acting reversible contraception (LARC)
- STI epidemiology in Finland: chlamydia trends (increasing); gonorrhea (increasing); HIV (stable)
- STI testing services: accessible, free, and confidential for under-18s in Finland
- Online sexual health resources: e-Loops; THL resources

## 3. Module TE2: Terveys ja ympäristö (Health and Environment)

### 3.1 Core Philosophy

This module examines health from a **community, social and environmental** perspective. It addresses how the surrounding world – physical, social, digital, and ecological – affects health and well-being.

### 3.2 Learning Objectives

Students will:
- Understand the health-environment relationship from a systemic perspective
- Analyse how social and cultural factors shape health behaviors
- Critically evaluate the health implications of environmental factors
- Connect individual health choices to community and planetary health (One Health)

### 3.3 Detailed Content

**Physical Environment and Health:**
- Air quality (ilmanlaatu): indoor and outdoor pollution; effects on respiratory and cardiovascular health
- Climate change and health: heat waves, extreme weather, vector-borne diseases (tick-borne diseases relevant in Finland), food security
- One Health framework: interconnection of human health, animal health, and ecosystem health
- Water quality; chemical exposure; environmental toxins

**The School Environment:**
- School as a health-promoting environment (terveydenedistävä koulu)
- Physical school environment: ergonomics, light, noise, indoor air quality
- Social school environment: school climate (kouluyhteisö); sense of belonging; bullying prevention
- **THL 2023**: 8–9% of basic education students and 1–4% of upper secondary students report weekly bullying
- Violence prevention in school: Finnish anti-bullying programs (KiVa Koulu – internationally recognized)

**Digital Environment and Health:**
- Social media and well-being: research on social comparison, FOMO, anxiety, body image
- Cyberbullying: prevalence in Finland; legal consequences; support services
- Digital health economy: mHealth apps; health data privacy; tracking and datafication of health
- Algorithmic influence on health behavior and information intake
- Gaming and online communities: positive potential (social connection, skills) vs. problematic use

**Social and Cultural Environment:**
- How socioeconomic status affects health access and outcomes in Finland
- Health-related inequalities by gender: girls' mental health crisis in Finnish THL data
- Multiculturalism and health: immigrant health; access to services; cultural factors
- Peer culture and social norms around health (drinking culture, diet ideals, fitness)

## 4. Module TE3: Terveys ja yhteiskunta (Health and Society)

### 4.1 Core Philosophy

The third module connects health to the **civic, political, economic, and ethical** dimensions of society. Students develop health citizenship – the ability to understand and participate in health-related decision-making at community, national and global levels.

### 4.2 Learning Objectives

Students will:
- Understand the structure and principles of the Finnish healthcare system
- Analyse health policies and ethical questions from multiple perspectives
- Evaluate research evidence and media representations of health
- Develop as active health citizens: advocacy, participation, rights

### 4.3 Detailed Content

**Finnish Healthcare System:**
- Structure: primary care (terveyskeskukset), specialist care (sairaanhoitopiirit → hyvinvointialueet since 2023), private care
- **Welfare area reform (hyvinvointialueuudistus, 2023)**: restructuring of Finnish social and healthcare services into 21 welfare areas
- Funding: tax-funded (sairausvakuutus, kela, municipal taxation)
- Patient rights: informed consent; right to information; confidentiality; language rights
- **YTHS** (Finnish Student Health Service): for lukio and university students; physical, mental, dental health

**Health Policy and Prevention:**
- Finnish national health programs: *Terveys 2015*, *Sosiaali- ja terveysministeriön strategia*
- Health promotion at population level: legislation (tobacco, alcohol, sugar tax discussions)
- The Sote reform 2023: implications for access to services; regional health inequalities
- Comparative health systems: Finland vs. other Nordic models vs. European models

**Research, Evidence and Ethics:**
- How scientific research is conducted; reading health studies critically
- Evidence-based medicine vs. pseudoscience: example case studies
- Media and health: health journalism; how news shapes public health behavior
- Big pharma and conflicts of interest: pharmaceutical marketing; over-medicalization
- **Bioethics**: end-of-life care; euthanasia debate in Finland; organ donation
- Ethics of genetic information: genetic testing; privacy; discrimination
- AI in healthcare: ethical challenges; diagnostic AI; data concerns

**Global Health Citizenship:**
- Global health: disease burden by world region; antimicrobial resistance globally
- Finnish development aid and global health equity
- UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs): health-related goals (SDG 3)
- WHO and international health governance; pandemic preparedness (COVID-19 lessons)

## 5. Ylioppilastutkinto (Matriculation Examination) in Health Education

### 5.1 Overview

Health education can be chosen as a **ylioppilaskoeaine** (matriculation exam subject). This is a distinctive feature of the Finnish system – few EU countries examine health education at the national exit exam level.

**Exam structure:**
- Format: Written exam, essay-based + structured questions
- Duration: 6 hours
- Frequency: Twice a year (spring and autumn)
- Examination board: **Ylioppilastutkintolautakunta (YTL)**
- Language: Finnish or Swedish

**Content of the exam:**
- Tests terveysosaaminen (health literacy) – not factual recall alone
- Requires ability to analyse, evaluate, and synthesize health information
- Current health issues and ethical dilemmas are part of the exam
- Requires knowledge of TE1, TE2, TE3 content AND ability to apply it

**Significance:**
- The existence of a national exam in health education signals the subject's **academic seriousness** in Finland
- It motivates students to study health education deeply and systematically
- This contrasts sharply with Ireland (no SPHE exam), Spain (no dedicated health exam), Portugal (Biologia yes, but not health ed per se), and Poland (no EZ exam)

## 6. Teacher Qualifications (Opettajan pätevyys)

Finnish health education teachers must meet specific qualifications – one of the strictest teacher qualification frameworks in the EU for this subject:

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Educational level | Master's degree (maisterin tutkinto) |
| Subject studies | ≥60 ECTS in health education subject studies (aineopinnot) |
| Pedagogical studies | ≥60 ECTS in teacher education (pedagogiset opinnot) |
| University providers | University of Jyväskylä (primary); University of Tampere (secondary) |
| Additional option | Liikunta + terveystieto double qualification (common combination) |

This is comparable to the requirement for physics or history teachers – health education is treated as a **rigorous academic discipline** in Finland, not as a life skills activity.
