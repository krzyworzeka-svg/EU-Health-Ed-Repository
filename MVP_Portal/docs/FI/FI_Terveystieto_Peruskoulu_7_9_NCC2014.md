# Finland – Terveystieto (Health Education): Curriculum Content in Peruskoulu (Grades 7–9), NCC 2014

## 1. Subject Overview

### 1.1 Purpose and Philosophy

*Terveystieto* (Health Education) in grades 7–9 of Finnish basic education is built on the concept of **terveysosaaminen** (health literacy). This goes far beyond factual knowledge of health topics – it encompasses:

- **Tiedot** (Knowledge): understanding health concepts, the body, disease, and the healthcare system
- **Taidot** (Skills): practical capabilities to promote one's own and others' health
- **Asenteet** (Attitudes): values, care for oneself and others, respect for diversity
- **Toimintavalmius** (Readiness to act): critical decision-making; seeking help when needed

> **Key principle: Assessment does NOT evaluate the student's personal values, attitudes, temperament, or health behaviors. Only health literacy (terveysosaaminen) is assessed.**

### 1.2 Position in the Curriculum

- **Mandatory** for ALL students in grades 7, 8, and 9
- Stand-alone, independent subject (not integrated into biology or PE)
- **Total hours**: determined by local curriculum (typically ~76 lessons over 3 years = approx. 2 lessons/week)
- Taught by qualified **health education teachers** (Master's degree + 60 ECTS in health education subject studies + 60 ECTS in pedagogical studies)

## 2. Content Area S1: Kasvu ja kehitys (Growth and Development)

### 2.1 Overview

This content area examines adolescent development from a holistic perspective – physical, emotional, psychological, and social. It recognizes the diversity and individual pace of development as natural.

### 2.2 Topics Covered

**Identity and Self-Knowledge:**
- Construction of personal identity during adolescence: who am I, what do I value?
- Self-image (minäkuva) and self-esteem (itsetunto): healthy vs. unrealistic self-image
- Media and social media influence on body image and identity
- Peer pressure and the need to belong vs. individual identity

**Sexual Development and Sexuality (Seksuaalinen kehitys):**
- Puberty: physical and emotional changes in girls, boys, and those with diverse gender identities
- The spectrum of sexual development: diversity and individual variation
- Sexual identity: heterosexuality, homosexuality, bisexuality, asexuality, questioning
- Gender identity: gender as a spectrum; transgender and non-binary identities; Finnish legal framework (Gender Recognition Act 2023)
- Sexual rights: the right to one's own body; the right to say no; age of consent in Finland (16 years)

**Emotional and Interaction Skills:**
- Recognizing and naming emotions (emotional literacy)
- Empathy: understanding others' perspectives and needs
- Communication in relationships: assertiveness, active listening
- Managing conflict constructively: mediation, compromise
- Loneliness and social exclusion: prevention and support

**Stress and Crisis:**
- Age-appropriate stress: academic pressure, social expectations, future anxiety
- Distinguishing normal stress from pathological stress
- Resilience (resilienssi): coping strategies; bounce-back ability
- Crises in adolescence: grief, family difficulties, trauma
- Where to seek help: school nurse, school psychologist, crisis lines (Sekasin-chat, Mieli ry)

## 3. Content Area S2: Terveyteen vaikuttavat tekijät (Factors Affecting Health)

### 3.1 Overview

This is the most extensive content area, covering both health-promoting and health-threatening factors. It has a strong preventive orientation while emphasizing positive health resources.

### 3.2 Lifestyle Factors (Elintavat)

**Nutrition (Ravitsemus):**
- Finnish Nutrition Recommendations (Valtion ravitsemusneuvottelukunta): principles of a balanced diet
- The Lautasmalli (plate model) and the nutrition wheel
- Eating habits of Finnish adolescents: breakfast skipping (significant problem)
- Critical analysis of food products and marketing
- Eating disorders (syömishäiriöt): anorexia, bulimia, binge eating; risk factors; seeking help
- Sustainable nutrition: environmental impact of food choices

**Sleep and Rest (Uni ja lepo):**
- Adolescent sleep needs: 8–10 hours recommended
- Consequences of sleep deprivation: academic performance, mental health, immune function
- Sleep hygiene: routines, screen use before bed, light exposure
- 2023 THL data: sleep improving but still significant issue for Finnish teens

**Physical Activity (Liikunta):**
- WHO recommendations: ≥60 min/day of moderate-to-vigorous activity
- Benefits of physical activity on mental health: depression, anxiety, self-esteem
- Sedentary behavior: screen time; independent of physical activity as a separate risk factor
- Finland's national programs: Liikkuva koulu (Schools on the Move) – movement-integrated school day
- Sports club participation vs. independent activity

**Mental Health (Mielenterveys):**
- Mental health as a resource: positive mental health (not just absence of disease)
- Common mental health challenges in adolescence: anxiety, depression, loneliness
- Warning signs and risk factors for mental health problems
- **THL 2023 data**: 31–34% of 8th–9th grade girls report moderate-to-severe anxiety
- Stigma and help-seeking: why young people don't ask for help; how to overcome barriers
- Mental health services available: school nurse, school psychologist, youth counseling (nuorisoasema), crisis lines

### 3.3 Sexual Health (Seksuaaliterveys)

**Contraception (Ehkäisy):**
- Methods of contraception: condom (primary + dual protection), hormonal methods (pill, ring, patch, implant, IUD), emergency contraception
- Effectiveness of different methods; typical vs. perfect use
- Access to contraception in Finland: school nurse (free condoms in many schools), health centers, sexual health clinics
- Finnish law: under-18s can access contraception and STI testing without parental consent

**STIs (Sukupuolitaudit / Seksitaudit):**
- Most common STIs in Finland: chlamydia (most common), gonorrhea, HPV, HIV/AIDS, herpes
- Transmission routes; symptoms and asymptomatic infections
- Testing and treatment: free STI testing available
- HPV vaccination: part of Finland's national vaccination program (girls since 2013, all since 2020)

**Sexual Violence and Harassment:**
- Definitions: sexual harassment, sexual assault, rape in Finnish law
- Online sexual harassment: grooming, sextortion sharing intimate images without consent
- Consent: what it means; how to communicate it; when it cannot be given
- Resources: SERI (Sexual Violence Support Centre); Nollalinja (for intimate partner violence)

### 3.4 Substance Use and Addictions (Päihteet ja Riippuvuudet)

**Alcohol:**
- Effects on the developing adolescent brain: neurological vulnerability under 25
- Finnish alcohol culture: context in Nordic society
- Binge drinking (*kertajuominen*): prevalence; acute risks (alcohol poisoning, accidents, risky behavior)
- Long-term health risks: liver disease, cancer, addiction
- Finnish law: under-18 cannot buy or consume alcohol

**Tobacco and Nicotine:**
- Cigarettes: composition; addiction (nicotine); health effects (lung cancer, COPD, cardiovascular disease)
- E-cigarettes and vaping pods: rapidly increasing use among Finnish teens (THL 2023: significant rise)
- Nicotine pouches (*nikotiinipussit*): new trend; regulation in Finland
- Finnish tobacco legislation: smoke-free spaces; age restrictions

**Cannabis:**
- Effects of cannabis on the adolescent brain: impaired memory, motivation, psychiatric risk
- Legal status in Finland: illegal (unlike Portugal's decriminalization)
- Trends: cannabis use in Finland lower than EU average (a matter of context for comparison)

**Behavioral Addictions:**
- Gaming disorder (peliriippuvuus): WHO ICD-11 criteria; Finnish gaming culture
- Social media addiction: FOMO, comparison loops, sleep disruption
- Internet addiction: problematic internet use; THL 2023: 10–16% report sleep/eating affected by internet use
- Gambling (rahapelaaminen): in Finland, Veikkaus monopoly + restriction system

### 3.5 Diseases and Accident Prevention (Sairaudet ja Tapaturmien Ehkäisy)

- Non-communicable diseases (NCD): type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, cancer – risk factors and prevention
- Infectious diseases: vaccination policy (Finland has high vaccination coverage); seasonal flu; COVID-19 as case study
- Antibiotic resistance: responsible use; Finnish context (Finland among lowest antibiotic users in EU)
- Accident prevention: road safety; sports injuries; home accidents
- First aid basics: CPR principles; calling 112; assisting in emergencies

## 4. Content Area S3: Terveys, yhteisöt ja yhteiskunta (Health, Communities and Society)

### 4.1 Overview

This content area places health in its social, cultural, community, and global context. It fosters health-related citizenship.

### 4.2 Topics Covered

**Environmental Health:**
- Physical environment: air quality; pollutants; climate change as a health issue (One Health connection)
- Social environment: school climate (*kouluyhteisö*); bullying (THL 2023: 8–9% report weekly bullying)
- Digital environment: online communities; digital well-being; cyberbullying

**Media Literacy and Health Information:**
- Critical evaluation of health information: how to assess reliability of sources
- Health misinformation and pseudoscience: identifying quackery; anti-vaccination claims
- Social media and health: influencers promoting products; body image content
- Evidence-based health information: THL, WHO, medical databases vs. commercial sources

**Use of Healthcare Services (Terveyspalvelut):**
- Finnish healthcare system: school health services (kouluterveydenhuolto); health centers (terveyskeskus); hospitals; emergency (112)
- Rights as a patient: free healthcare for minors; confidentiality
- Student health services (opiskeluterveydenhuolto)
- Mental health services: student counseling; crisis services; psychiatric services

**Human Rights and Health:**
- Health as a fundamental right (Finnish Constitution; UN Declaration)
- Health inequalities: socioeconomic factors; gender differences; ethnic minority health
- Global health: international health organizations (WHO, UNICEF); global disease burden

## 5. Pedagogical Approach in Finnish Health Education

### 5.1 Core Pedagogical Methods

| Method | Application in Terveystieto |
|---|---|
| **Inquiry-based learning** | Students research health questions and present findings |
| **Collaborative learning** | Group work on health scenarios; peer discussion |
| **Discussion and debate** | Structured discussion on ethical health dilemmas |
| **Case analysis** | Anonymous or fictional health situations |
| **Project-based learning** | Community health projects; school well-being projects |
| **Critical media analysis** | Analysis of health advertising, social media content |
| **Reflection and journaling** | Personal health reflection (not assessed for content) |

### 5.2 Transversal Competencies (Laaja-alainen osaaminen)

Terveystieto contributes to all 7 transversal competence areas of the Finnish NCC 2014:
1. **Thinking and learning to learn** (L1)
2. **Cultural competence, interaction, self-expression** (L2)
3. **Taking care of oneself, managing daily life** (L3) ← most directly linked
4. **Multiliteracy** (L4) ← media literacy in health
5. **ICT competence** (L5)
6. **Working life competence and entrepreneurship** (L6)
7. **Participation, involvement, sustainable future** (L7)
