# Finland – Terveystieto: FAQ, Implementation Context and 6-Country Comparative Analysis (2025)

## 1. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### 1.1 Structure and Curriculum

**Q: Is there a dedicated "Health Education" subject in Finland?**  
A: Yes – **Terveystieto** (Health Education) is a **mandatory, standalone, independent subject** in Finnish schools:
- Grades 7–9 of peruskoulu (basic education, ages 13–16): obligatory for all pupils
- Lukio (general upper secondary, ages 16–19): 3 mandatory modules (TE1, TE2, TE3)
- This makes Finland one of the very few EU countries with dedicated, mandatory health education at both lower and upper secondary level.

**Q: When did Finland introduce health education as a separate subject?**  
A: Finland's parliament passed legislation in **2001** making Terveystieto a mandatory independent subject. Implementation began in all schools in **August 2004**. The motivation was concern about worsening adolescent health trends and recognition that integrating health content into other subjects was insufficient.

**Q: What qualifications do health education teachers need in Finland?**  
A: Finnish health education teachers are required to hold:
- **Master's degree** (5 years)
- **≥60 ECTS** in health education subject studies (aineopinnot)  
- **≥60 ECTS** in pedagogical studies (pedagogiset opinnot)

This is equivalent to the requirement for physics or chemistry teachers – Health Education is treated as a rigorous academic subject, not a life skills activity.

**Q: Can health education be examined in the Finnish Matriculation Examination (Ylioppilastutkinto)?**  
A: Yes. Health Education is an **optional** subject in the ylioppilastutkinto (Baccalaureate-level national examination). Students who choose it must demonstrate health literacy – not just recall facts, but analyse, evaluate and apply health knowledge. This is unique in Europe.

**Q: Are personal health behaviors assessed in Finnish Terveystieto?**  
A: Explicitly **no**. The national curriculum states:
> *"Assessment shall not target the student's personal values, attitudes, temperament, or health behaviors."*

Only health literacy (terveysosaaminen) is assessed – the ability to understand and critically analyse health phenomena.

### 1.2 Specific Topics

**Q: How is sexual health education delivered in Finland?**  
A: Sexual health is integrated into Terveystieto across all levels:
- Grades 7–9 (S2): anatomy, puberty, contraception, STIs, sexual diversity, consent, online safety
- Lukio TE1: sexual health as a lifelong resource; advanced contraception; STI epidemiology; healthy relationships
- There is NO separate law required (unlike Portugal's Lei 60/2009 or France's law on 3 sessions). It is included in regular Terveystieto curriculum.
- School nurses in many schools provide **free condoms** to students.

**Q: How does Finland address the mental health crisis among girls?**  
A: The THL Kouluterveyskysely 2023 revealed that **31–34% of 8th–9th grade girls** report moderate-to-severe anxiety – a dramatically higher rate than boys (7–8%). Finland's response includes:
- School psychologist (koulupsykologi) and social worker (koulukuraattori) in every school
- **National Mental Health Strategy 2020–2030** (STM)
- Low-threshold services: Sekasin-chat (anonymous online mental health chat for youth)
- 24/7 crisis line: 116 123 (Mieli ry)
- This is addressed directly in Terveystieto curriculum (mental health as central theme)

**Q: What is Finland's approach to bullying prevention?**  
A: Finland developed **KiVa Koulu** – an internationally recognized, evidence-based anti-bullying program used in ~90% of Finnish basic education schools. THL 2023 data show bullying increased slightly (8–9% weekly bullying in grades 8–9), highlighting remaining challenges despite the program.

**Q: How does the Finnish healthcare system support school health education?**  
A: Finland has an integrated school healthcare system (kouluterveydenhuolto):
- Mandatory health examinations in grades 1, 5, 8 (peruskoulu) and 1st year of lukio
- School nurse (terveydenhoitaja) available daily, free, confidential
- School doctor for more complex issues
- All of this is **free and universal** – no socioeconomic barriers to access

## 2. Context of the Finnish Model (Historical and Policy)

### 2.1 Why Finland Introduced Terveystieto as a Separate Subject

The 2001 parliamentary decision was motivated by:
- **Research showing** that health content integrated into biology and PE was insufficient for developing health literacy
- **Worsening adolescent health trends** in the 1990s (substance use, mental health)
- **International context**: WHO recommendations for comprehensive health education
- **Finnish educational philosophy**: all important competencies deserve dedicated subject status

### 2.2 The Nordic Welfare State Context

The Finnish model of health education CANNOT be separated from the broader Nordic welfare state:
- **Universal healthcare**: all students have access to free school health services and general healthcare
- **Free contraception for minors**: available through the healthcare system
- **Social safety net**: reduces health disparities linked to poverty
- **Teacher autonomy**: high-quality teachers with high salaries and academic status
- **Evidence-based policy**: government decisions informed by THL data

### 3.3 Finland's Distinctive Strengths

1. **The ONLY country in this 6-country panel** where health education is: 
   - A mandatory, standalone subject at BOTH lower AND upper secondary level
   - Can be examined at the national exit examination (ylioppilastutkinto)
   - Requires teachers with subject-specific Master's degree qualifications

2. **World-class school health infrastructure**: daily school nurse, school doctor protocol, school psychologist and social worker – all free and universal

3. **KiVa Koulu** – one of the world's best-evidenced anti-bullying programs, embedded in schools and connected to health education

4. **Liikkuva koulu** (Schools on the Move) – unique national program integrating movement throughout the school day; more evidence-based than typical PE-alone approaches

5. **National biennial health monitoring** (THL Kouluterveyskysely, 260,000 students) – provides granular, school-level data to guide both policy and individual school action

6. **Assessment ethics**: explicit legal protection from assessing personal values and health behaviors – unique in Europe

### 3.4 Challenges and What Finland Can Learn from Others

**Finland's challenges:**
- **Mental health crisis among girls**: 31–34% moderate-to-severe anxiety (THL 2023) – despite excellent services
- **Rising e-cigarette use**: curriculum and prevention programs need updating
- **Bullying still present**: KiVa Koulu helps, but THL 2023 shows small increase
- **Physical activity inequality by gender**: girls significantly less active than boys at all ages

**What Finland could learn from other countries:**
- **Ireland's Junior Cycle SPHE 2023**: highly structured, modular approach to sexual health and LGBTQI+ topics – could update TE1 structure
- **France's EVARS 2025**: specific, detailed programme for sexuality/relationships/pornography topics – more explicit structure than current Finnish practice
- **Portugal's UCC model**: integration of health workers from community health centers into schools (Finland's school nurses are in-school, but inter-agency collaboration could be stronger)
