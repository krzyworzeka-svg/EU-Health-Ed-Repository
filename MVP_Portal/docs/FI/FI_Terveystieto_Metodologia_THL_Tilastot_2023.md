# Finland – Terveystieto: Methodology, School Health Services and Youth Health Statistics (2023–2025)

## 1. Pedagogical Philosophy of Finnish Health Education

### 1.1 Constructivist and Participatory Approach

Finnish health education pedagogy is rooted in **constructivism** (tiedon rakentaminen): students are not passive receivers of health information but active builders of health literacy. Key principles:

- **Student-centered** (oppilaslähtöisyys): starting from students' own experiences, questions, and needs
- **Experiential** (kokemusperäinen): learning from real-life health situations
- **Critical** (kriittinen): questioning, evaluating, and analysing health information rather than accepting it uncritically
- **Values-based** (arvopohjaissuus): developing personal responsibility, ethical reflection, respect for others
- **Participatory** (osallistava): students contribute to designing learning environments and school well-being

### 1.2 Health as a Positive Resource (Salutogenesis)

Rather than focusing primarily on risks and diseases, Finnish health education uses a **salutogenic** orientation:
- What makes people healthy and resilient?
- What resources (voimavarat) do young people have?
- How can strengths be built on?

This contrasts with purely pathogenic models that list diseases and dangers.

### 1.3 Assessment Ethics

One of the most distinctive features of Finnish health education assessment:

> **"Arviointi ei saa kohdistua oppilaan henkilökohtaisiin arvoihin, asenteisiin, temperamenttiin tai terveystottumuksiin."**
> *(Assessment shall not target the student's personal values, attitudes, temperament, or health behaviors.)*

This means:
- A student who smokes is **not penalised** in assessment for that behavior
- A student who holds traditional views on sexuality is **not penalised** for those views
- What is assessed is the **ability to understand, analyse and critically evaluate** health phenomena
- This protects student privacy and avoids coercive or surveillance-based health education

## 2. Key Pedagogical Methods in Terveystieto

| Method | Description | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **Tutkiva oppiminen** (Inquiry-based learning) | Students formulate questions, research, and present health topics | High |
| **Yhteistoiminnallinen oppiminen** (Cooperative learning) | Group investigation of health cases | High |
| **Arvokeskustelu** (Values discussion) | Structured dialogue on ethical health dilemmas; no "right answer" imposed | High |
| **Tapaustutkimus** (Case study analysis) | Analysis of fictional or anonymized health situations | High |
| **Median analyysi** (Media analysis) | Critical examination of health advertising, social media content, news | Medium |
| **Projektityö** (Project work) | Community health projects; school well-being surveys | Medium |
| **Simulaatio** (Simulation/role play) | Communication in health situations; help-seeking scenarios | Medium |
| **Reflektiivinen kirjoittaminen** (Reflective writing) | Personal health reflection journals (not assessed for content) | Low-medium |
| **Asiantuntijavierailu** (Expert visits) | School nurse, doctor, psychologist, peer educator | Variable |
| **Liikkuva opetus** (Movement-integrated learning) | Physical activity integrated into lessons | Growing |

## 3. School Health Services in Finland

### 3.1 Kouluterveydenhuolto (School Health Care System)

Finland has one of the best-developed school health care systems in Europe. It is **free, universal, and integrated** into the school environment:

| Service | What it provides |
|---|---|
| **Terveydenhoitaja** (School nurse) | Regular health checks; confidential consultations; sexual health advice; contraception; STI testing referrals; free condoms in many schools |
| **Lääkäri** (Doctor) | Obligatory health examinations (1st, 5th, 8th grade and lukio 1st year); specialist referrals |
| **Koulupsykologi** (School psychologist) | Mental health support; psychological assessments; crisis intervention |
| **Koulukuraattori** (School social worker) | Social support; family issues; preventing exclusion |
| **Erityisopettaja** (Special education teacher) | Learning difficulties; SEND support |

**Mandatory health examinations (terveystarkastukset):**
- Grades 1, 5, 8 (peruskoulu) + 1st year of lukio: comprehensive exam by school nurse + doctor
- All other years: school nurse examination
- All examinations include assessment of physical health, mental well-being, and social factors

### 3.2 YTHS (Opiskeluterveydenhuolto for Lukio)

For lukio and higher education students:
- **YTHS** (Ylioppilaiden terveydenhoitosäätiö – Finnish Student Health Service) provides healthcare for higher education students
- For lukio students: school-based terveydenhuolto + additionally municipal health services

## 4. Key National Programs Related to Health Education

### 4.1 Liikkuva koulu (Schools on the Move)

- **National program** launched 2010, now universal across Finland
- Aims to increase physical activity during the school day (breaks, active transport, movement-integrated lessons)
- Connected to health education: demonstrates the value of physical activity experientially
- **Target**: every student gets at least 60 minutes of physical activity during the school day
- Evidence base: improves academic performance, concentration, and well-being

### 4.2 KiVa Koulu (KiVa Anti-Bullying Program)

- **Internationally recognized Finnish anti-bullying program** developed at University of Turku
- Used in 90% of Finnish basic education schools
- Involves all school staff + trained student groups (*KiVa teams*)
- Contains elements of social skills, empathy, and health literacy relevant to Terveystieto
- Connected to THL data: bullying prevention effectiveness measured by Kouluterveyskysely

### 4.3 Nuorisoasema (Youth Counseling Centers)

- Municipal services (youth counseling, mental health, substance abuse) for young people
- Low-threshold: no referral needed; anonymous possible
- Complementary to school health services

## 5. Youth Health Statistics: THL Kouluterveyskysely 2023

The **Kouluterveyskysely** (School Health Promotion Study) is Finland's largest national health survey of young people, conducted by THL every 2 years. The 2023 wave reached approximately **260,000 students**.

### 5.1 Mental Health (2023)

| Indicator | Grades 8–9 | Upper Secondary / Vocational | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Girls reporting moderate-to-severe anxiety | **31–34%** | **31–34%** | Significantly higher than boys |
| Boys reporting moderate-to-severe anxiety | 7–8% | 7–8% | Gender gap is striking |
| Students with good/excellent perceived health | ~80% (most) | ~80% | Overall still majority positive |
| Weekly bullying experiences (victims) | **8–9%** | 1–4% | Increased vs. 2021 |
| Sleep < 8h on school nights (% declining) | Improving | Improving | Positive trend vs. 2021 |

> **Key finding**: The **gender gap in anxiety** is one of the most striking findings. Girls' mental health in Finland has deteriorated significantly over the last decade, much more so than boys'. This is a national health emergency addressed by multiple policies.

### 5.2 Physical Activity (2023)

| Indicator | Grades 8–9 | Upper Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Meeting WHO daily 60 min recommendation | ~20–25% (boys) | Declining with age |
| Girls meeting recommendation | ~15% | Further decline |
| Significant increase in physical activity (2021→2023) | ✅ Positive trend | ✅ Most groups |
| Screen time affecting sleep/eating | 10–16% report this | 10–16% |

### 5.3 Substance Use (2023)

| Substance | Grades 8–9 | Upper Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Alcohol (ever tried) | Declining: ~30–40% | ~60–70% |
| Regular alcohol use | Declining | Declining |
| **E-cigarettes/vaping** | **Increasing significantly** | **Major upward trend** |
| Cannabis (ever tried) | <10% | ~15% |
| Nicotine pouches (*nikotiinipussit*) | New; emerging concern | New |

> **Key finding**: While traditional alcohol and cigarette use has been **declining**, **e-cigarette and nicotine pouch use is rising rapidly** among Finnish teens – a major emerging issue for health education.
