# Ireland – SPHE Senior Cycle Specification (2024)

## 1. Introduction and Purpose

### 1.1 What is Senior Cycle SPHE?

Social, Personal and Health Education (SPHE) at Senior Cycle is a **mandatory, non-examined** subject delivered in the final two years of secondary education (5th and 6th Year, ages ~15–18). The new specification, published in September 2024, replaces fragmented previous provision and creates for the first time a **unified, consistent Senior Cycle SPHE curriculum** for all students.

### 1.2 Why Was a New Specification Needed?

Prior to 2024, Senior Cycle SPHE provision was:
- **Inconsistent** across schools – some provided it, many did not
- **Not specified** in detail – only general guidelines existed (2006)
- Delivered differently depending on school ethos, resources, and teacher availability
- **Disconnected** from the more structured Junior Cycle provision

The reform responds to:
- Evidence of poor mental health outcomes among Irish adolescents (HBSC 2022: nearly 50% of teens reporting low mood)
- The wider **Senior Cycle Redevelopment** programme (2022 onwards)
- Growing consensus about the need for consistent RSE (Relationships and Sexuality Education) provision at Senior Cycle
- Demand from students, parents, teachers, and health professionals

### 1.3 Key Structural Facts

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Total hours | **60 hours** over 2 years of Senior Cycle (5th and 6th Year) |
| Publication | September 2024 |
| Mandatory for LCA students | **September 2025** |
| Mandatory for all Leaving Cert students | **September 2027** |
| Examination | **Not examined** in Leaving Certificate |
| Number of strands | **3 main strands** |
| Cross-cutting elements | **3 elements** threading through all strands |
| Connection to LC reform | Part of broader Senior Cycle Redevelopment; "Cultivating Wellbeing" is one of seven key competencies |

## 2. Curriculum Structure – Three Strands

### Strand 1: Health and Wellbeing

This strand helps students develop the knowledge, understanding, skills, and dispositions to **support their physical, emotional, social, and spiritual wellbeing**. It emphasises self-care, resilience, health literacy, and the ability to navigate health systems.

**Key topics and learning areas:**

**Physical health:**
- Understanding the relationship between lifestyle choices and long-term health
- Nutrition and healthy eating for adults – evidence-based guidance vs. diet culture myths
- Physical activity: recommended levels, benefits for mental and physical health
- Sexual and reproductive health: contraception, STIs, sexual health services
- Preventive healthcare: screening, check-ups, vaccination as adult responsibility

**Mental and emotional health:**
- Understanding mental health as a continuum, not a binary
- Resilience and positive psychology: strengths, purpose, gratitude
- Stress management strategies appropriate for students under exam pressure
- Sleep and its critical role in cognitive function, mood, and health
- Recognising and responding to anxiety, depression, eating disorders
- Emotional regulation and self-compassion

**Social health:**
- Social determinants of health: how socioeconomic factors affect wellbeing
- Health inequalities in Ireland and globally
- Community, belonging, and social connectedness as health factors
- Volunteering and civic participation as wellbeing resources

**Health literacy:**
- How to access, understand, and evaluate health information
- Navigating the Irish health system (GP, Emergency Department, mental health services, HSE)
- Rights and responsibilities as a health consumer
- Critical evaluation of health claims in media, advertising, and online

*Representative learning outcomes:*
- Students will be able to develop and implement a personal health plan addressing their own identified needs
- Students will be able to describe the Irish health service landscape and identify appropriate pathways to care
- Students will be able to critically evaluate health information from a range of sources
- Students will be able to demonstrate strategies for building and maintaining positive mental health

---

### Strand 2: Relationships and Sexuality

This strand takes a **holistic, rights-based approach** to relationships and sexuality, building on the foundation established in Junior Cycle SPHE. It encourages students to develop healthy, respectful relationships both in-person and online, while understanding sexuality as a positive dimension of human identity.

**Key topics and learning areas:**

**Consent and communication:**
- Understanding consent: active, ongoing, freely given, reversible, informed
- Communication skills in intimate relationships: negotiating boundaries, expressing needs
- The legal framework around consent in Ireland (Sexual Offences Act 2017)
- Coercive control: recognising patterns of controlling behaviour in relationships
- What to do if consent is violated: supports, reporting, legal rights

**Relationships in the digital age:**
- Online relationships: safety, authenticity, respect
- Sexting and digital intimacy: legal dimensions, peer pressure, consequence
- Cyberbullying in the context of relationships: reporting and support
- The role of pornography: influence on attitudes, expectations, and body image
- Dating apps: responsible use; recognising predatory behaviour

**Sexuality and identity:**
- Human sexuality as a spectrum: understanding sexual orientation and gender identity
- LGBTQ+ identities: respectful language, inclusion, and Irish legal context
- Fertility and reproductive health in young adults
- Contraception: types, effectiveness, availability in Ireland
- Sexually transmitted infections (STIs): prevention, testing, treatment
- Pregnancy options in Ireland: up-to-date information including legal context post-2018

**Relationships and violence:**
- Intimate partner violence: forms, impact, support services
- Sexual violence and abuse: definitions, prevalence, responding and reporting
- Safe relationships: recognising healthy vs. unhealthy dynamics in adulthood

*Representative learning outcomes:*
- Students will be able to explain the concept of consent and apply it reflectively to varied relationship scenarios
- Students will be able to identify signs of abusive relationships, including emotional, physical, sexual, and coercive control
- Students will be able to describe the range of contraceptive methods available, their effectiveness, and where to access them in Ireland
- Students will be able to demonstrate understanding of diverse sexual orientations and gender identities with respectful and inclusive language

---

### Strand 3: Into Adulthood

This strand focuses on **preparing students for the transition to life beyond school** – into higher education, the workforce, independent living, and adult citizenship. It nurtures life skills, self-advocacy, and the ability to navigate societal structures.

**Key topics and learning areas:**

**Transitions and future pathways:**
- Post-school options: higher education (CAO system), further education, apprenticeships, employment
- Managing change, uncertainty, and the stress of transition
- Building a positive identity beyond academic results
- Financial literacy: budgeting, managing money as a young adult, understanding debt and credit
- Tenants' rights, accommodation options, homelessness awareness

**Citizenship and society:**
- Democratic participation: voting, activism, community involvement
- Understanding Irish and European political systems
- Social justice and human rights: engaging with inequality
- Climate change as a health and justice issue; individual and collective action
- Media literacy and misinformation in a democratic society

**Work and wellbeing:**
- Rights of workers in Ireland: employment law basics, contracts, trade unions
- Managing work-life balance as a young adult
- Recognising workplace stress, bullying, and harassment
- The role of meaningful work in wellbeing and identity

**Independent living:**
- Self-care in independent contexts (cooking, managing health appointments, exercise)
- Mental health supports for young adults: accessing counselling, online tools, services
- Alcohol and substance use in adult social contexts: informed decision-making
- Building and maintaining supportive social networks in adulthood

*Representative learning outcomes:*
- Students will be able to create a realistic personal financial plan for an early adult scenario
- Students will be able to identify their roles and responsibilities as a citizen in a democratic society
- Students will be able to locate and access appropriate supports for their own health and wellbeing needs post-school
- Students will be able to evaluate the factors that contribute to a meaningful and healthy adult life

## 3. Cross-Cutting Elements

Three cross-cutting elements thread through all three strands:

### Element 1: Thinking Critically and Empathetically

Students are supported to:
- Question assumptions and social norms
- Consider multiple perspectives, including those of people with different identities and experiences
- Apply ethical reasoning to health and social decisions
- Develop empathy as a skill and disposition

### Element 2: Exercising Rights, Responsibilities, and Inclusivity

Students are encouraged to:
- Understand their rights in health, relationships, and civic life
- Recognise corresponding responsibilities to self and others
- Contribute to inclusive environments in school, online, and in society
- Challenge discrimination and exclusion in any form

### Element 3: Being Health Literate

Students develop the ability to:
- Access and evaluate health information from multiple and varied sources
- Navigate health and social services effectively
- Communicate health needs clearly to professionals and others
- Make evidence-informed decisions about their own health

## 4. Connection to Senior Cycle Redevelopment

### 4.1 Seven Key Competencies

The Senior Cycle Redevelopment programme defines **seven key competencies** to be developed across all subjects. SPHE is centrally aligned to "**Cultivating Wellbeing**" but also contributes to all others:

| Competency | SPHE Contribution |
|---|---|
| Thinking and solving problems | Critical evaluation of health information; ethical dilemmas |
| Being creative | Personal projects; creative approaches to wellbeing |
| Communicating | Assertiveness; consent conversations; advocacy |
| Working with others | Collaborative activities; peer support |
| Participating in society | Citizenship; volunteering; democratic engagement |
| **Cultivating wellbeing** | **Core focus of SPHE across all 3 strands** |
| Managing learning and self | Emotional regulation; self-advocacy; future planning |

### 4.2 Connection to SPHE at Junior Cycle

Senior Cycle SPHE **builds upon** the foundations of Junior Cycle SPHE (2023 spec), ensuring a **coherent 5-year SPHE journey** from 1st Year to 6th Year in post-primary school. Students build progressively on earlier knowledge and skills rather than repeating content.

---

## 5. Assessment

Senior Cycle SPHE is **not part of the Leaving Certificate examination**. It is:
- **Non-examined** – no terminal written exam
- Reflected through **classroom engagement and formative assessment**
- May include: projects, discussions, reflective journals, presentations
- Does not contribute to Leaving Certificate points calculation
- However, its **mandatory status** ensures it is tracked and reported by schools

---

## 6. Parental Opt-Out Provisions

Consistent with the Education Act and Irish policy tradition:
- **Parents** may request that their child be withdrawn from specific content that contravenes their religious or moral convictions
- **Students aged 18+** may decide independently about their own participation
- Schools must **inform parents** of the content of the SPHE programme
- Schools are encouraged to engage parents proactively and provide information note opportunities
