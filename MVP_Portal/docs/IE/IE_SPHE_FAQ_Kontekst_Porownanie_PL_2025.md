# Ireland – SPHE: Implementation Context, FAQ and Comparative Analysis (2025)

## 1. Context of SPHE Reform in Ireland

### 1.1 Why Is SPHE Being Reformed Now?

Ireland is in the midst of a **comprehensive curriculum reform** at all levels of education. SPHE is being updated as part of this broader effort, but also in response to specific pressures:

**Health pressures:**
- Adolescent mental health deterioration: HBSC 2022 found nearly **50% of Irish teenagers report low mood** – more than doubling since 1998
- Rising rates of anxiety and depression among 12–18-year-olds
- Increasing rates of **overweight and obesity**: ~17.7% of primary school-age children (2022–23)
- Youth alcohol use, vaping, and cannabis experimentation remaining high
- Significant gender gap in mental health outcomes – girls more affected than boys

**Social and technological pressures:**
- Social media and smartphones creating new mental health challenges (FOMO, cyberbullying, body image)
- Pornography increasingly accessible to under-18s, influencing relationship expectations
- Online misinformation about health, bodies, and sexuality
- Irish society becoming more diverse – SPHE must be inclusive of many backgrounds

**Educational pressures:**
- Inconsistency of previous SPHE delivery (especially at Senior Cycle) – no guarantee students received any RSE or mental health education
- Junior Cycle reform (2015) created the Wellbeing Programme, requiring more structured SPHE
- Senior Cycle reform (2022) demanded a new mandatory SPHE specification
- Post-COVID need to address gaps in student social-emotional learning

**Legislative and policy drivers:**
- Health (Regulation of Termination of Pregnancy) Act 2018 – Ireland legalised abortion; SPHE required to provide up-to-date, accurate information about reproductive health options
- Government's *Healthy Ireland: A Framework for Improved Health and Wellbeing 2019–2025*
- Department of Health's *National Drugs Strategy 2017–2025*
- Growing advocacy from student unions (USI, ISSU) for better RSE and mental health education

## 2. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### 2.1 General Questions

**Q: Is SPHE compulsory in Ireland?**  
A: Yes. SPHE is **mandatory** at both Junior Cycle (3 years) and Senior Cycle (2 years). At Junior Cycle, the updated specification has been mandatory since September 2023. At Senior Cycle, the new 2024 specification is mandatory for LCA students from September 2025 and for all Leaving Cert students by September 2027.

**Q: Does SPHE appear on the Leaving Certificate?**  
A: No. Senior Cycle SPHE is **not examined** and does not contribute to CAO points. However, it is a mandatory curriculum component that schools must deliver and document.

**Q: Is SPHE the same as sex education?**  
A: SPHE is broader than sex education. RSE (Relationships and Sexuality Education) is **one strand** of SPHE (Strand 3 at Junior Cycle; Strand 2 at Senior Cycle). SPHE also covers mental health, making healthy lifestyle choices, substance use, civic preparation, and transition to adulthood.

**Q: Can parents withdraw their child from SPHE?**  
A: Parents may request withdrawal from **specific content** that contravenes their moral or religious convictions – this applies specifically to content touching on sexuality and reproduction. They cannot withdraw a child from SPHE entirely. When students reach age 18, they decide independently.

**Q: How many hours per year does SPHE take?**  
A: Junior Cycle: approximately 33 hours per year (100 hours over 3 years). Senior Cycle: approximately 30 hours per year (60 hours over 2 years).

### 2.2 Content-Related Questions

**Q: Does SPHE cover mental health?**  
A: Yes, comprehensively. **Strand 4 (Emotional Wellbeing)** at Junior Cycle covers: emotional literacy, stress management, depression, self-harm (safe messaging), crisis response, resilience, and seeking help from services. Senior Cycle Strand 1 (Health and Wellbeing) deepens this with evidence-based coping strategies, mental health service navigation, and adult transitions.

**Q: Does SPHE cover LGBTQ+ topics?**  
A: Yes. Both Junior and Senior Cycle specifications include LGBTQ+ identities explicitly. This includes understanding sexual orientation, gender identity, respectful language, and the legal context (Ireland has recognised same-sex marriage since 2015 and gender recognition since 2015). Organisations like **BeLonG To** provide school resources.

**Q: Does SPHE address addiction and substance use?**  
A: Yes. At Junior Cycle, alcohol, tobacco, cannabis, and other substances are covered in **Strand 2 (Making Healthy Choices)** including effects on the developing brain. At Senior Cycle, students revisit substance use in the broader context of adult decision-making and health literacy.

**Q: Is pornography discussed in SPHE?**  
A: Yes. The 2023 Junior Cycle specification explicitly includes the **influence of pornography on attitudes, behaviours, and relationship expectations** as a learning outcome within the Relationships and Sexuality strand. This was a deliberate, research-informed addition to address a growing issue affecting Irish young people.

**Q: Does Senior Cycle SPHE address the topic of abortion?**  
A: Yes. Since Ireland changed its law in 2018, SPHE is expected to provide **factually accurate and non-judgemental information** about pregnancy options available in Ireland, including access to termination services. This is part of reproductive health content in Strand 2.

**Q: Is One Health or environmental health part of SPHE?**  
A: Not explicitly as a dedicated area – unlike the Polish model (Edukacja Zdrowotna Dział VIII). However, **Strand 3 (Into Adulthood)** at Senior Cycle touches on **climate change as a health and justice issue**, and health literacy broadly includes environmental health awareness. This is less developed than in the Polish curriculum.

### 2.3 Teacher and School Questions

**Q: Who delivers SPHE?**  
A: Any qualified teacher can deliver SPHE. In practice, it is most commonly taught by Guidance Counsellors, Home Economics teachers, PE teachers, or Biology teachers. Unlike in Poland, there is no single mandatory qualification pathway for SPHE teachers.

**Q: Does every school have an SPHE teacher?**  
A: The Department of Education requires all post-primary schools to provide SPHE. However, the reality varies – not all schools have a dedicated SPHE teacher, and provision can vary significantly depending on school ethos, resources, and principal leadership.

**Q: What training supports SPHE teachers?**  
A: **Oide** (the national teacher professional development service) is the primary support. They provide workshops, online resources, cluster support, and specialist materials. Completion of training is not mandatory but strongly encouraged.

**Q: Can a school choose not to teach SPHE?**  
A: No – SPHE is mandatory. However, schools have autonomy over how they timetable and deliver it, and oversight is primarily through school self-evaluation (SSE) rather than inspections focused solely on SPHE.

## 4. Comparative Analysis: Ireland (SPHE) vs. Poland (Edukacja Zdrowotna)

### 4.1 Structural Comparison

| Feature | Ireland (SPHE) | Poland (Edukacja Zdrowotna) |
|---|---|---|
| Subject name | Social, Personal and Health Education | Edukacja Zdrowotna |
| First introduced (post-primary) | 2000 (Junior Cycle) | **2025** |
| Current key specification year | 2023 (JC) / 2024 (SC) | 2025 |
| Mandatory? | Yes (JC + SC) | Yes |
| Total hours (post-primary) | 160 hours (100 JC + 60 SC) | ~120–140 hours total |
| Is it examined? | No (neither JC nor SC) | No (no Leaving Cert equivalent) |
| Number of thematic areas | 4 strands (JC) / 3 strands (SC) | **11 areas (one curriculum)** |
| Oversight body | NCCA | MEN + ORE |
| Teacher support body | Oide | ORE + ZPE |
| Digital resources | Spunout, HSE website, Oide resources | ZPE (dedicated digital platform) |

### 4.2 Thematic Coverage Comparison

| Health Theme | Ireland SPHE | Poland EZ |
|---|---|---|
| Mental health / Emotional wellbeing | ✅ Dedicated strand (JC Strand 4; SC Strand 1) | ✅ Dział V |
| Relationships and sexuality | ✅ Dedicated strand (JC Strand 3; SC Strand 2) | ✅ Dział VII |
| Physical health / Healthy choices | ✅ JC Strand 2 | ✅ Dział II |
| Nutrition and diet | ✅ Within JC Strand 2 | ✅ Dedicated Dział IV |
| Physical activity | ✅ PE separate (400h Wellbeing programme) | ✅ Dedicated Dział III |
| Substance use and addiction | ✅ JC Strand 2 | ✅ Dedicated Dział IX |
| Social health and relationships | ✅ All strands | ✅ Dział VI |
| Environmental / Planetary health | ⚠️ Partial (SC Strand 3 only) | ✅ Dedicated Dział VIII (incl. One Health) |
| Health system literacy | ✅ SC Strand 1 | ✅ Dedicated Dział X |
| Citizenship / Into adulthood | ✅ Dedicated (SC Strand 3) | ⚠️ No dedicated dział; embedded |
| Digital health / Media literacy | ✅ Cross-cutting theme | ✅ Included in Dział IX |
| Values and attitudes | ✅ Embedded throughout | ✅ Dedicated Dział I |
| Dojrzewanie / Puberty | ✅ JC Strand 3 (Year 1) | ✅ Only at primary (not post-primary) |

### 4.3 Key Similarities

1. **Both are mandatory** at post-primary level
2. **Both are non-examined** – no high-stakes assessment
3. **Both adopt holistic definitions of health** (bio-psycho-social models)
4. **Both emphasise active, participatory learning methods**
5. **Both include RSE/sexuality education** as a core component
6. **Both require whole-school wellbeing approaches**
7. **Both use national bodies** for curriculum development and teacher support (NCCA/ORE)

### 4.4 Key Differences

| Aspect | Ireland | Poland |
|---|---|---|
| **Longevity** | 25 years of SPHE provision (since 1999/2000) | **Brand new from 2025** |
| **Environmental health** | Limited; Poland has stronger One Health framing | One Health (Planetary Health) as explicit dział |
| **Opt-out rights** | Parents have right to opt out of specific RSE content | No opt-out – fully mandatory |
| **Digital platform** | Various partner sites (no single ZPE-equivalent) | Dedicated ZPE platform with all materials |
| **Teacher qualifications** | No single required pathway; any teacher+CPD | Defined pathway (biology/WF + postgraduate diploma in EZ) |
| **Citizenship strand** | Explicit (SC Strand 3: Into Adulthood, + CSPE) | Less explicit; embedded in social health dział |
| **Interconnection with health system** | Strong (SC Strand 1: navigating Irish HSE) | Strong (Dział X: system ochrony zdrowia) |

### 4.5 Irish Good Practices Worth Considering for Polish EZ

1. **Spiral curriculum design** across 5+ years of post-primary – Poland could deepen 11-dział structure progressively across all 4 years of liceum
2. **Explicit "Into Adulthood" strand** – preparing students for independent adult life (financial literacy, tenants' rights, job rights) not yet prominent in Polish EZ
3. **400-hour Junior Cycle Wellbeing Programme** – embedding wellbeing across SPHE + CSPE + PE in an interconnected way
4. **Safe messaging formalisation** – Ireland's mandatory safe messaging for suicide/self-harm content is a model for Poland
5. **Dedicated organisations** (Spunout, BeLonG To, Jigsaw) providing independent, student-facing resources that complement classroom SPHE
6. **Parental opt-out procedure** – Ireland's structured, limited opt-out framework may be a useful model for navigating Polish parental concerns

### 4.6 Polish EZ Good Practices Worth Considering for Irish SPHE

1. **One Health / Planetary Health** as an explicit area (Dział VIII) – stronger than Ireland's approach
2. **Dedicated ZPE digital platform** – centralised, nationally consistent digital resources for all schools
3. **Mandatory teacher qualification pathway** – more rigorous and standardised than Ireland's
4. **Dedicated Nutrition dział** – Ireland covers nutrition within broader "Making Healthy Choices" but less systematically
5. **System ochrony zdrowia (health system literacy)** – Poland's Dział X is comprehensive; Ireland covers it partially in SC Strand 1

## 5. Policy Context: Healthy Ireland

### 5.1 Healthy Ireland Framework (2019–2025)

SPHE sits within Ireland's national health promotion strategy:
- **Goal**: *"A healthier Ireland with wellbeing at its centre"*
- Builds on original Healthy Ireland Framework (2013)
- Identifies schools as key settings for health promotion
- SPHE is a core delivery mechanism for school-based health promotion

Key pillars relevant to SPHE:
1. Healthy eating and active living
2. Mental health and wellbeing
3. Tobacco, alcohol, and drug reduction
4. Sexual health and crisis pregnancy prevention

Link: **https://www.gov.ie/en/publication/healthy-ireland/**

### 5.2 National Drugs Strategy (2017–2025)

Ireland's drug strategy (*Reducing Harm, Supporting Recovery*) explicitly identifies schools and SPHE as key prevention settings. SPHE substance use content must align with:
- Evidence-based harm reduction messaging
- Age-appropriate information
- Signposting to support services (HSE Drugs helpline: 1800 459 459)
