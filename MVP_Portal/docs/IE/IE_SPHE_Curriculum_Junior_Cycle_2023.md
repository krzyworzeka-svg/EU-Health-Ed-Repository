# Ireland – SPHE Junior Cycle Specification (2023)

## 1. Introduction and Purpose

### 1.1 What is Junior Cycle SPHE?

Social, Personal and Health Education (SPHE) is a **mandatory subject** at Junior Cycle in Irish post-primary schools (years 1–3, ages ~12–15). The updated specification, implemented from September 2023, replaces the previous 2000 SPHE specification.

### 1.2 Aims of the Subject

The Junior Cycle SPHE specification aims to:
- **Build self-awareness and positive self-worth** in students
- Develop the **knowledge, skills, and values** necessary for students to lead fulfilling and healthy lives
- **Empower students** to create and maintain respectful relationships with others
- **Enhance students' capacity** to contribute positively to the world around them
- Promote healthy decision-making and critical thinking about health-related topics
- Provide a safe and supportive learning environment where students can explore personal and social issues

### 1.3 Key Structural Facts

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Total hours | **100 hours** over 3 years of Junior Cycle (up from 70 hours in previous spec) |
| Year of introduction | September 2023 (mandatory for incoming 1st Year students) |
| Assessment | **Not formally examined** at Junior Cycle Final Examination; assessed through classroom-based work |
| Timetable | Approximately 1 class per week (~70 min/week or equivalent) |
| Delivery | Typically by a dedicated SPHE teacher; whole-school approach also required |
| Number of strands | **4 main strands** |

## 2. Curriculum Structure – Four Strands

### Strand 1: Understanding Myself and Others

This strand focuses on self-awareness, identity, and interpersonal understanding. Key themes include:

**Year 1 (age ~12–13):**
- Exploring personal identity and individuality
- Understanding emotions and emotional intelligence
- Building self-esteem and positive self-concept
- The importance of support networks: family, friends, community
- Recognising strengths and areas for growth

**Year 2 (age ~13–14):**
- How personal beliefs, values, and attitudes are shaped
- Understanding stereotypes, bias, and prejudice
- Building empathy and perspective-taking
- Communication in relationships – verbal and non-verbal
- Navigating peer pressure and social influence

**Year 3 (age ~14–15):**
- Personal development and emerging adulthood
- Exploring identity in a digital age
- Understanding diversity and inclusion in Irish society
- Civic identity and belonging to communities
- Reflecting on personal growth over Junior Cycle

*Representative learning outcomes:*
- Students should be able to identify and describe personal values and how they influence decisions
- Students should be able to demonstrate empathy and active listening skills
- Students should be able to reflect on how social media affects self-perception and identity

---

### Strand 2: Making Healthy Choices

This strand addresses physical health, lifestyle choices, substances, and health literacy. Key themes include:

**Year 1 (age ~12–13):**
- Understanding what health means: physical, mental, emotional, social dimensions
- Sleep, nutrition, and physical activity as foundations of health
- Screen time and digital wellbeing – healthy habits online
- Introduction to food, diet, and body image

**Year 2 (age ~13–14):**
- Alcohol: facts, risks, societal attitudes; legal age and advertising
- Tobacco and vaping (e-cigarettes): health risks, addiction and dependence
- Drugs – including cannabis and new psychoactive substances (NPS/'headshop drugs')
- How to refuse substances – refusal skills and assertiveness
- Understanding addiction as a health issue

**Year 3 (age ~14–15):**
- Effects of substances on the developing brain and long-term health
- Gambling and gaming: understanding problematic behaviours
- Access to health services in Ireland – where to get help
- Critical evaluation of health information online and in media
- First aid and emergency response awareness

*Representative learning outcomes:*
- Students should be able to analyse advertising and peer influence on health-related choices
- Students should be able to describe the short and long-term effects of alcohol and tobacco on the body
- Students should be able to identify trusted sources of health information and support services

---

### Strand 3: Relationships and Sexuality

This strand takes a **holistic, rights-based, and age-appropriate** approach to relationships and sexuality. It builds progressively over the three years of Junior Cycle.

**Year 1 (age ~12–13):**
- What makes a healthy relationship: respect, trust, communication, kindness
- Friendships: forming, maintaining, and navigating difficulties
- Puberty and physical development: understanding the changes
- Personal boundaries – saying no; rights in relationships
- Introduction to online relationships and digital communication safety

**Year 2 (age ~13–14):**
- Understanding consent: what it means in friendships and relationships
- Romantic relationships: feelings, expectations, pressure
- Understanding gender: biological sex, gender identity, and social roles
- Sexual orientation and diversity: respectful language and understanding
- Healthy and unhealthy relationship behaviours; recognising early warning signs

**Year 3 (age ~14–15):**
- Sexual health: understanding reproduction and contraception at an age-appropriate level
- Sexually transmitted infections (STIs): key facts and prevention
- Influence of pornography on attitudes, behaviours, and relationship expectations
- Online grooming and exploitation: recognising and reporting
- Developing healthy attitudes to sexuality as a part of human identity

> **Note:** The Department of Education has explicitly stated that SPHE content is **age-appropriate and evidence-based** and does not include graphic or explicit material.

*Representative learning outcomes:*
- Students should be able to explain what consent means and why it is central to healthy relationships
- Students should be able to identify resources and supports for issues related to sexual health and relationships
- Students should be able to critically evaluate the impact of pornography and media stereotypes on relationship expectations

---

### Strand 4: Emotional Wellbeing

This strand focuses on mental health, emotional literacy, and coping skills. Key themes include:

**Year 1 (age ~12–13):**
- Understanding emotions: naming, expressing and regulating feelings
- Stress and anxiety: recognising signs and constructive coping strategies
- Importance of sleep and rest for emotional wellbeing
- Building resilience: bouncing back from setbacks
- School belonging and connection as protective factors

**Year 2 (age ~13–14):**
- Mental health: what it is; challenging stigma
- Understanding depression and anxiety: signs and symptoms
- Self-harm and suicidal ideation: how to recognise and respond safely (safe messaging guidelines)
- Seeking help: talking to trusted adults; professional support
- Online wellbeing: social media, cyberbullying, and digital resilience

**Year 3 (age ~14–15):**
- Positive psychology: strengths, gratitude, purpose, hope
- Grief, loss, and bereavement: understanding and responding
- Understanding eating difficulties and body image
- Mindfulness and other evidence-based strategies for emotional regulation
- Transition and change: moving from Junior to Senior Cycle

> **Key principle:** SPHE adopts **safe messaging guidelines** when addressing topics such as suicide, self-harm, and mental health crisis – following guidance from the National Office for Suicide Prevention (NOSP) and HSE.

*Representative learning outcomes:*
- Students should be able to demonstrate techniques to manage stress and anxiety
- Students should be able to describe warning signs of a mental health difficulty in themselves or others and know how to seek help
- Students should be able to explain how personal wellbeing can be protected and supported

## 3. Cross-Cutting Themes

Across all four strands, the specification embeds key cross-cutting themes:

| Theme | Application |
|---|---|
| **Digital literacy** | Evaluating online health information; online relationships; social media influence |
| **Equality and inclusion** | LGBTQ+ inclusion; diversity; disability awareness; anti-discrimination |
| **Human rights** | Rights in relationships; right to safety; right to health information |
| **Critical thinking** | Media literacy; advertising; misinformation |
| **Whole-school wellbeing** | SPHE connects to broader school wellbeing policy and culture |

## 4. Learning Outcomes Framework

The specification uses a **learning outcomes** model (consistent with broader Junior Cycle reform). Each learning outcome describes what the student should know, understand, and be able to do at the end of Junior Cycle.

### Sample Learning Outcomes by Strand

**Strand 1 – Understanding Myself and Others:**
> "Appreciate how personal values and beliefs influence attitudes and behaviour"

> "Examine how stereotypes and prejudice affect individuals and society"

**Strand 2 – Making Healthy Choices:**
> "Explore the short and long-term effects of alcohol on a person's health and wellbeing"

> "Demonstrate skills to make healthy choices in relation to physical and mental health"

**Strand 3 – Relationships and Sexuality:**
> "Understand the concept of consent in the context of all relationships"

> "Evaluate the influence of pornography on attitudes, expectations, and behaviours in relationships"

**Strand 4 – Emotional Wellbeing:**
> "Identify strategies to cope with stress and emotional difficulties"

> "Know where to seek help if they or someone they know is experiencing a mental health difficulty"

## 5. Assessment

SPHE at Junior Cycle is **not assessed through formal examinations**. Assessment is:
- **Formative / classroom-based** – ongoing feedback during learning
- Focused on participation, reflection, and skills development
- Schools may use well-designed tasks, discussions, creative projects, reflective journals
- No Classroom-Based Assessment (CBA) requirement (unlike some other Junior Cycle subjects)
- Recorded in the **Junior Cycle Profile of Achievement (JCPA)** as a subject completed

## 6. Teacher Qualifications and Professional Development

### 6.1 Teacher Requirements

SPHE is typically taught by a teacher with a background in:
- Guidance Counselling
- Home Economics
- Physical Education
- Biology / Science
- Or any teacher who has completed SPHE professional development training

> There is no single mandatory SPHE-specific qualification at Junior Cycle level; rather, **professional development** through Oide is expected.

### 6.2 Oide Professional Learning

**Oide** (the merged professional development service) provides:
- Face-to-face workshops on implementing the new spec
- Online courses and webinars
- School cluster supports
- Resources for SPHE planning and delivery

Resources available at: **https://oide.ie/post-primary/home/wellbeing/sphe-junior-cycle/**

## 7. Comparison with Previous (2000) Specification

| Feature | Previous (2000) Spec | New (2023) Spec |
|---|---|---|
| Hours | 70 hours over 3 years | **100 hours** over 3 years |
| Strands | 3 modules | **4 strands** |
| RSE | Separate RSE curriculum | **Integrated** as Strand 3 |
| Digital wellbeing | Not explicitly included | **Explicit strand focus** |
| Emotional Wellbeing | Limited coverage | **Dedicated Strand 4** |
| Safe messaging | Not formalised | **Embedded (NOSP guidelines)** |
| Learning outcomes model | Objectives-based | **Learning outcomes model** |
| Mental health focus | Basic | **Comprehensive** |
