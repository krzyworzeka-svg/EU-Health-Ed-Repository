# Ireland – SPHE: Legal Framework and Regulatory Context (2023–2025)

## 1. Overview of the Irish Education System

### 1.1 Key Facts

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Country | Ireland (IE) |
| Population | ~5.1 million (2024) |
| Official languages | Irish (Gaeilge), English |
| Education system type | Centralised curriculum, decentralised delivery (school autonomy on ethos) |
| Compulsory schooling age | 6–16 years |
| Primary school | Junior Infants (age 4/5) to 6th Class (age 11/12) – 8 years |
| Junior Cycle (post-primary) | 1st Year to 3rd Year (age 12–15) – 3 years; leads to Junior Cycle Profile of Achievement (JCPA) |
| Senior Cycle (post-primary) | 4th Year (Transition Year, optional) + 5th–6th Year (age 15–18) – 2 years; leads to Leaving Certificate |
| Curriculum authority | National Council for Curriculum and Assessment (NCCA) |
| Department responsible | Department of Education Ireland |

### 1.2 Governance Structure

The Irish education system operates through:

- **Department of Education** – sets national policy, statutory legislation, funding
- **NCCA (National Council for Curriculum and Assessment)** – develops and reviews curriculum specifications; advises the Minister
- **Oide** – professional learning and development service for teachers (formed from merger of PDST, JCT, and other services in 2022)
- **Schools** – implement curriculum with significant autonomy regarding ethos, timetabling, and implementation approach
- **Parents** – retain the right to opt children out of specific content that contravenes their conscience

## 2. History of SPHE in Ireland

### 2.1 Origins (1999–2010)

| Date | Development |
|---|---|
| 1999 | SPHE introduced at primary level as part of the revised primary curriculum |
| 2000–2001 | SPHE introduced at post-primary Junior Cycle level |
| 2003 | RSE (Relationships and Sexuality Education) established as part of SPHE |
| 2006 | Senior Cycle SPHE guidance published (non-mandatory, guidelines only) |

### 2.2 Junior Cycle Reform (2015–2023)

| Date | Development |
|---|---|
| 2015 | Framework for Junior Cycle published – Wellbeing designated as a key pillar |
| 2015–2017 | Junior Cycle Wellbeing programme introduced, incorporating SPHE, CSPE, PE |
| 2019 | NCCA begins review of Junior Cycle SPHE specification |
| 2022 | New Junior Cycle SPHE specification approved |
| September 2023 | New Junior Cycle SPHE specification implemented for all first-year students |

### 2.3 Senior Cycle Reform (2022–2027)

| Date | Development |
|---|---|
| 2022 | Department of Education announces Senior Cycle reform programme |
| 2023–2024 | NCCA develops new Senior Cycle SPHE specification |
| September 2024 | New Senior Cycle SPHE specification published and available for voluntary adoption |
| September 2025 | Mandatory for Leaving Certificate Applied (LCA) students |
| September 2027 | Mandatory for all Senior Cycle students (Leaving Certificate Established) |

### 2.4 Primary Curriculum Reform (2023–2026)

| Date | Development |
|---|---|
| 2023 | Primary Curriculum Framework published by NCCA |
| September 2025 | New Wellbeing specification (integrating SPHE + PE) published; rollout begins 2025/26 |
| 2026–2027+ | Phased enactment in schools (Wellbeing must be one of first three areas adopted) |

## 4. SPHE within the Wider Wellbeing Framework

### 4.1 Junior Cycle Wellbeing Programme

SPHE at Junior Cycle is part of the broader **Junior Cycle Wellbeing Programme**, which requires:
- **400 hours** of Wellbeing across three years of Junior Cycle
- Delivered through: SPHE, CSPE (Civic, Social and Political Education), PE, and additional components
- Schools required to demonstrate a whole-school approach to wellbeing

**Six Wellbeing Indicators** that the programme aims to develop:

| Indicator | Description |
|---|---|
| **Active** | Leading a physically active life; engaged in learning |
| **Responsible** | Making healthy choices; taking responsibility for learning and actions |
| **Connected** | Feeling connected to school, community, and people |
| **Resilient** | Coping with challenges and setbacks |
| **Respected** | Feeling valued; respecting others |
| **Aware** | Being aware of own thoughts and feelings; understanding their impact |

### 4.2 Senior Cycle Wellbeing as Key Competency

In the Senior Cycle reform, **"Cultivating Wellbeing"** is one of seven key competencies integrated across ALL revised subject specifications:

1. Thinking and solving problems
2. Being creative
3. Communicating
4. Working with others
5. Participating in society
6. **Cultivating wellbeing** ← SPHE central to this
7. Managing learning and self

### 4.3 Parental Opt-Out Rights

Under Irish law and education policy:
- Parents **retain the right** to request a child be withdrawn from specific SPHE content that contravenes their moral or religious convictions
- Once a student reaches **age 18**, they decide independently on their participation
- This provision specifically applies to RSE (Relationships and Sexuality Education) content
- Schools are required to inform parents of the SPHE programme content

## 5. Role of Key Institutions

| Institution | Role in SPHE |
|---|---|
| **NCCA** (National Council for Curriculum and Assessment) | Develops and revises SPHE specifications; provides guidelines and support materials |
| **Department of Education** | Sets mandatory status, implementation timelines, policy |
| **Oide** (Professional Development Service for Teachers) | Delivers professional learning for SPHE teachers; prints CPD resources |
| **TUSLA** (Child and Family Agency) | Child protection framework relevant to mandatory reporting; links to SPHE safeguarding content |
| **HSE** (Health Service Executive) | Health promotion programmes supporting SPHE delivery; online resources |
| **FSAI / HIQA** | Food/health authority materials relevant to health-related SPHE content |
| **Schools** | Implement SPHE within own ethos and timetable |
