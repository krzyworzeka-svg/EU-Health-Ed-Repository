# Ireland – SPHE Methodology, Wellbeing Framework and Teacher Support (2023–2025)

## 1. Pedagogical Philosophy of Irish SPHE

### 1.1 Guiding Principles

The Irish SPHE curriculum is built on a set of core pedagogical principles that distinguish it from more traditional, content-heavy subjects:

**1. Student-Centred Learning**
SPHE places the student's own experiences, questions, and needs at the centre of learning. The curriculum deliberately moves away from purely teacher-led instruction toward facilitated discussion, inquiry, and reflection.

**2. Spiral Curriculum Design**
Themes introduced at primary level re-emerge and deepen at Junior Cycle, then again at Senior Cycle. Students revisit core concepts (e.g., consent, mental health, healthy choices) with increasing complexity and sophistication at each stage.

**3. Holistic Understanding of Health**
Following the WHO definition, Irish SPHE treats health as encompassing:
- Physical wellbeing
- Mental and emotional wellbeing
- Social wellbeing
- Spiritual/existential wellbeing (in age-appropriate ways)

**4. Rights-Based Approach**
The curriculum is grounded in children's and human rights frameworks. Students learn about their rights in relationships, in healthcare settings, as citizens, and in digital spaces.

**5. Evidence-Based Practice**
Content is developed in line with:
- WHO guidance on health education
- NCCA research on adolescent development
- HBSC (Health Behaviour in School-aged Children) Ireland data
- National clinical guidelines (HSE, NOSP)

**6. Safe Learning Environment**
Given the sensitivity of many SPHE topics, the curriculum explicitly requires teachers to:
- Establish **ground rules** at the outset (group agreement/contract)
- Create **psychological safety** so students can engage honestly
- Use **distancing techniques** – discussing issues in third person or through scenarios to avoid personal disclosure pressure
- Follow **safe messaging guidelines** (for suicide, self-harm, eating disorders)

## 2. Recommended Teaching Methods

### 2.1 Active and Participatory Learning

NCCA and Oide recommend moving beyond lecture formats to **active learning approaches**:

| Method | Description | Suitable for... |
|---|---|---|
| **Discussion circles** | Structured group discussions with ground rules | Exploring values, relationships, emotional topics |
| **Case studies / scenarios** | Analysis of realistic situations (names changed, fictionalised) | Consent, substance use, peer pressure situations |
| **Role play and simulation** | Acting out situations and practising responses | Refusal skills, communication, consent |
| **Debate and structured controversy** | Exploring two sides of health/social issues systematically | Advertising, alcohol policy, social media |
| **Reflective journaling** | Private writing about personal responses and insights | Emotional wellbeing, identity, values |
| **Gallery walks** | Moving around classroom stations with prompts and posters | Generating discussion on multiple topics simultaneously |
| **Fishbowl discussion** | Small group discusses while others observe then swap | Deep discussion on sensitive topics |
| **Think-Pair-Share** | Individual reflection, partner discussion, group sharing | Warm-up to sensitive topics; reducing anxiety |
| **Project-based learning** | Students research and create outputs (videos, campaigns) | Health promotion; citizenship strand |
| **Guest speakers** | Professionals (psychologist, nurse, youth worker) sharing expertise | Credibility; reducing stigma |

### 2.2 Distancing Techniques (Critical for Sensitive Topics)

When dealing with topics like mental health, sexual health, or substance use, teachers are trained to:
- Use **anonymised case studies** rather than asking for personal disclosures
- Frame discussion as: *"What might someone your age be thinking/feeling in this situation?"*
- Use **art, music, film, literature** as indirect entry points to personal topics
- Deploy **question boxes** – anonymous written questions students can submit

### 2.3 Technology and Digital Tools

Recommended digital tools for SPHE delivery:
- **HSE.ie resources** – interactive health content for students
- **Childline online resources** – for mental health strand
- **Spunout.ie** – peer-written mental health content for Irish young people
- **BeLonG To resources** – LGBTQ+ inclusive content
- **NCCA Curriculum Online** – lesson ideas and planning guides
- **Jigsaw.ie** – whole-school mental health programme resources

## 3. The Junior Cycle Wellbeing Programme

### 3.1 Overview

The **Junior Cycle Wellbeing Programme** is a mandatory whole-school programme (not just one subject) that frames SPHE within a broader context of student flourishing.

**Time allocation: 400 hours** of Wellbeing across three years of Junior Cycle

This 400 hours is delivered through:
- **SPHE** – 100 hours (updated specification 2023)
- **CSPE** (Civic, Social and Political Education) – 100 hours
- **Physical Education (PE)** – 100 hours
- **Additional Wellbeing provision** – 100 hours (school-designed: e.g., mindfulness, yoga, career guidance sessions, Transition Year preparation, etc.)

### 3.2 Six Wellbeing Indicators

The programme is structured around developing six wellbeing indicators in students:

| Indicator | What it looks like in practice |
|---|---|
| **Active** | Students are physically engaged; participate willingly in school activities and learning |
| **Responsible** | Students make thoughtful decisions; show accountability for their choices |
| **Connected** | Students feel they belong to the school community and have meaningful relationships |
| **Resilient** | Students can bounce back from adversity; they have coping strategies and supports |
| **Respected** | Students feel heard and valued by peers, teachers, and staff; they respect others |
| **Aware** | Students have self-awareness about their thoughts, feelings, and impact on others |

### 3.3 Whole-School Wellbeing Approach

SPHE is most effective when embedded in a **whole-school culture of wellbeing**. This means:
- Wellbeing is everyone's responsibility – not just the SPHE teacher
- School leadership models positive wellbeing (staff wellbeing matters too)
- Physical environment supports wellbeing (access to green space, quiet areas, safe corridors)
- Anti-bullying policies (Bí Cineálta guidelines) are actively implemented
- School uses **School Self-Evaluation (SSE)** to assess and improve wellbeing outcomes
- Parents are engaged as partners in supporting student wellbeing

## 4. Senior Cycle Wellbeing – Cultivating Wellbeing as a Key Competency

### 4.1 Embedding Wellbeing Across the Senior Cycle

In the Senior Cycle Redevelopment (2022 onwards), **"Cultivating Wellbeing"** is one of seven key competencies expected of all Senior Cycle students, across **all** subjects. This means:

- Teachers of ALL subjects are expected to be mindful of student wellbeing
- Subject specifications include explicit competency descriptors
- SPHE is the **primary subject** for this competency but it is not SPHE's exclusive domain

**"Cultivating Wellbeing"** competency descriptors for Senior Cycle students:
> *"Students develop self-awareness and self-management; they practise self-care and look after their physical and mental health; they show empathy and care for others; they reflect on their values and aspirations and manage stress effectively."*

### 4.2 SPHE's Unique Role

While wellbeing is whole-school, SPHE provides:
- The **dedicated space** for explicit discussions about health, relationships, and personal development
- **60 structured hours** for students to develop knowledge and skills they may not encounter elsewhere
- A **structured relationship between teacher and class group** in a non-examination context
- Opportunity for students to develop **health literacy** in a focused, deliberate way

## 5. Assessment Approach in SPHE

### 5.1 Principles of SPHE Assessment

SPHE assessment is fundamentally **formative and process-oriented**:
- The goal is to support student learning and growth, not to rank or grade
- **No terminal written examination** at either Junior or Senior Cycle
- Teachers use assessment **to inform their teaching**, not solely to report on students

### 5.2 Examples of Assessment Methods Used

| Method | Description |
|---|---|
| Reflective journal | Students record personal responses, learning, and insights over the year |
| Portfolio | Collection of work demonstrating engagement and growth |
| Discussion contributions | Quality of participation in group discussions |
| Case study analysis | Written or oral analysis of a health or social scenario |
| Personal health project | Independent or group research project on a chosen health topic |
| Peer assessment | Students give constructive feedback to each other on projects |
| Self-assessment | Students rate their own learning and identify areas of development |

### 5.3 What Is Avoided in SPHE Assessment

- Assessing **personal beliefs or values** (these are not assessable)
- Grading **emotional responses** or degree of personal disclosure
- **Ranking** students against each other
- **High-stakes memory testing** of factual content
- Assessing **lifestyle choices** outside the school setting

## 6. Teacher Qualifications and Professional Development

### 6.1 Who Teaches SPHE?

SPHE may be taught by any qualified teacher, but strong practice is supported by teachers with backgrounds in:
- **Guidance Counselling** – experience in personal development work
- **Home Economics** – health and nutrition expertise
- **Physical Education** – wellbeing and body awareness background
- **Biology** – knowledge of human health and reproduction
- **Religious Education** – experience with values and ethical questions (though SPHE is secular)

### 6.2 Role of Oide (Professional Development Service)

**Oide** is the merged professional development service for Irish teachers (formed 2022 from PDST, JCT and others). For SPHE, Oide provides:

**For Junior Cycle SPHE:**
- National rollout workshops for teachers on the 2023 specification
- Strand-specific resources and lesson exemplars
- Support for school cluster groups
- Guidance on safe messaging when discussing sensitive topics

**For Senior Cycle SPHE:**
- Introductory workshops on the new 2024 specification
- Deep-dive CPD on Strand 2 (Relationships and Sexuality) – most complex area
- Online modules accessible at any time
- School visit support for SPHE coordinators

Oide SPHE resources: **https://oide.ie/post-primary/home/wellbeing/**

### 6.3 Safe Messaging Training

All SPHE teachers are encouraged to complete training in:
- **Safe messaging on suicide and self-harm** – following HSE/NOSP guidelines
- **Child protection procedures** – mandatory for all teachers (Tusla reporting obligations)
- **Inclusive SPHE delivery** – particularly for LGBTQ+ content (BeLonG To training)
